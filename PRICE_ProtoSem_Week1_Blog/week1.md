# PRICE ProtoSem — Week 1
## From Strangers to a Squad

> We entered the room as strangers. One week later, I walked out knowing most of them—and with a lot of new friends.

### Before PRICE: Entering the Forge

As an Engineering student, I was asked to report for ProtoSem three days before the official PRICE opening. On August 12, I stepped into the KCT Tech Park—the Forge—and joined technical sessions being conducted for students from domains such as PRISM and ASADI.

The first session was Mechanical Designing, handled by Sabarish. He introduced us to Fusion 360. Since I already knew a little Blender, moving into another 3D design environment was easier than expected.

The morning was about installation, exploration and basics. In the afternoon, we were asked to choose an image from Pinterest and recreate it using clay. I was sitting at the back, so by the time the clay reached me, I had roughly half a bar of pink clay left. I improvised and made an APC. The next assignment was to recreate the same model digitally in Fusion 360.

The following day moved into intermediate sketching, solid tools, constraints and actual part design using a dimensioned reference drawing. Later, we had a five-minute paper-airplane challenge with someone we had not spoken to before. The goal was simple: fly straight and travel the longest distance. The homework was to design an aircraft while considering aerodynamics.

Friday continued with Fusion 360, including appearances and material properties.

Then Monday arrived.

# Day 1 — Breaking the Seal

We entered the room as strangers.

I was sitting with my CSE friends. After some time, we were asked to introduce ourselves—not just our names and departments, but what we were good at and what we could contribute.

I introduced myself as **Buvanesh S., Third Year CSE**, and said that I was good at **leveraging AI tools and Game Development**.

Gowri Shankar then conducted a Rock-Paper-Scissors activity. The purpose was not the game itself. It was to break the seal of strangers, interact and collaborate.

In the afternoon, we were asked to think of three problem statements we wanted to solve and propose possible solutions.

The message was clear: do not just sit and learn. Start thinking.

# Day 2 — Knowing Yourself Is Not Enough

Day 2 started with a LinkedIn onboarding session. We learned how to build a professional presence and officially added the experience of **Innovation Engineer Trainee — Full-time, On-site Internship**.

Then came the 16Personalities assessment. I got **ENFJ-T, the Protagonist**.

For the Zen Pencils storytelling activity, I chose Amy Poehler’s **“Great People Do Things Before They’re Ready.”** Its message was that trying something gives you information about yourself, even when the result is failure.

> I enter the battlefield with my curiosity as my weapon, and it will turn my experience into my armor.

My takeaway was simple:

**Knowing yourself is one thing. Knowing how to pitch yourself is another.**

# Day 3 — Beta Team Formation

Day 3 was the final goodbye to my CSE classmates before the Beta Teams were formed. I was expecting something like Task Force 141. After interacting with the new group, it felt more like the Angry Birds meme—but everyone was genuinely nice, and we had a lot of fun.

We learned what a Tech Talk is: take something emerging, something interesting, or something you know and explain it so that others can learn from you. Forge4Fauj and iDEX were used as examples.

Then came Among Us. Our team was Team 5 and Team 4 were the imposters. In one round our task was to sit back and relax, which we intentionally used to make ourselves suspicious. The imposters over-suspected people, which became their tell. We caught them, but majority vote decided the result.

Then came the Marshmallow Challenge:

- 20 spaghetti sticks
- 1 marshmallow
- 1 piece of thread
- 18 minutes

We spent most of our time planning and decoding the tape constraint. Then I looked at the clock.

**Thirty seconds.**

We abandoned the perfect plan and built a quick conical structure. We pierced the marshmallow, added supports, and somehow it stood.

**41 cm.**

Bent, improvised, but standing.

# Day 4 — The Longest Night in Gotham

I suspected the scheduled Tech Talk speaker might not show up, so I told my friend Linga that I needed a backup topic.

I chose **The Art of Prompting**.

The slides were not good enough, so I presented without depending on them. I explained four practical foundations:

**Role · Context · Task · Constraints**

I also shared my idea of using consistent AI personas such as **JARVIS** and **K.A.I.R.A.**, and gave a quick guide on which AI tool to use for what.

The official time limit was seven minutes for the presentation and three minutes for Q&A.

My version became **nearly fifteen minutes**.

The Longest Night in Gotham.

Some people at the back were fighting sleep, but several KCLAS students later told me the session was useful. Many of them had openly said on Day 1 that they were starting with very little technical exposure, yet they were willing to learn. I wanted to share what I knew with them.

Hema ma’am then guided us through the Forge’s core values, expectations, do’s and don’ts.

That afternoon, the official PRICE ProtoSem inauguration took place. **Kumar Rajagopalan, CEO of RAI (Retailers Association of India)**, shared perspectives that took the discussion beyond the immediate programme. One of the ideas captured from the session was the **PAFE framework: Personalised, Accurate, Fast, Everywhere.**

# Day 5 — Learning Beyond Engineering

August 21 was World Entrepreneurs’ Day.

We attended an entrepreneurship session alongside BBA students’ 24-hour sprint. **Swathi Sree**, founder of A Toddler’s Thing, and **Ramakrishnan Muthu**, Managing Director of Thulasi Pharmacy India Pvt. Ltd., shared their journeys.

Muthu sir’s story stayed with me: starting as a medical representative and eventually becoming an MD.

It was a reminder that a starting point is not a destination.

We ended the week by making our weekly recap and reflection. Gowri said: no template, use memes, make it fun.

So we did.

The leaderboard added another layer of chaos, especially around negative points and the -5 for informed leave.

But even those debates became part of the experience.

# One Week Later

When I walked into the room on Day 1, I had my CSE friends beside me.

Everyone else was a stranger.

By the end of the week, that had changed.

I had met people from different departments and backgrounds. I had worked with a new squad. I had shared something I knew with people who wanted to learn it. I had experimented with design, teamwork, storytelling, communication and entrepreneurship.

I had built an APC from half a bar of pink clay.

I had built a 41 cm spaghetti tower in thirty seconds.

I had delivered a fifteen-minute presentation that was supposed to last ten.

And somewhere between all of that, the strangers stopped being strangers.

> **Week 1 was not about becoming better at one particular skill. It was about discovering how many different ways there are to learn.**

I entered ProtoSem with my CSE friends.

I ended Week 1 with a new squad, new friends, new experiences, and a much clearer idea of the engineer I want to become.
